There is a joke I have heard from more than one Brazilian investor.

*"In Brazil, the past is unpredictable."*

They mean the exchange rate, the interest rate, the tax rules. Things you thought were settled can change. Brazilians are some of the sharpest investors I meet because they have had to be. They grew up with inflation, currency swings and the highest interest rates in the major economies. They know how to read risk.

And more and more, when they look for a place to put some of that capital outside Brazil, they are looking at the UAE.

## The story: two countries that love a big idea

Brazil and the UAE have more in common than people think. Both love scale. Both build things that look impossible on paper. Brasília was a capital city drawn on a blank map. Dubai was a fishing and trading town that became a global city in a single generation.

The connection is getting stronger every year. Emirates flies non-stop between São Paulo and Dubai. The UAE joined BRICS in 2024, putting the two countries in the same economic club. And Brazilian businesses, from agribusiness to tech, increasingly use the UAE as a door to the Middle East, Africa and Asia.

My own story runs through the Caribbean and Latin America. I am Jamaican by way of Cuba and Panama, and Saint Lucian by way of Guyana, and I grew up in London. Brazil is the giant at the centre of that region. When Brazilians move, the whole continent notices.

A Brazilian who moved R$1 million into dollars in 2015 would have had about USD 300,000. Leave that money in reais and by 2025 it bought closer to USD 180,000. That is the currency risk Brazilian families live with.

The UAE dirham is pegged to the US dollar at 3.6725 and has been since 1997. So a Dubai property is, in practical terms, a dollar asset. It does not remove your exposure to the real, because your life is still priced in reais, but it gives you a hard-currency asset that pays rent.

### 2. Yields that compete with Brazil's interest rates

Brazilians are spoiled by high domestic rates. When the Selic is in double digits, a 4% rental yield in Lisbon or Miami looks pointless. Why take property risk for less than the CDI?

That is why the UAE stands out. Dubai's average gross yields have run around **6 to 7%** in 2026, in a dollar-linked currency, with no personal income tax in the UAE. Add potential capital growth and the comparison with a real-denominated fixed income product becomes a genuine debate, not an easy win either way.

Two honest caveats:

- Yields are gross. Service charges and management typically take 1 to 2 points off.
- Yields have been compressing as prices rise faster than rents. Unit selection matters.

You can see how I filter projects in [how I choose off-plan units](/blog/how-i-choose-off-plan-units).

### 3. Safety

Ask Brazilian families what they want most from a second base and safety is near the top of every list. In Numbeo's 2026 index, Abu Dhabi ranked first in the world in the January edition and Dubai was in the top ten.

A Brazilian friend put it best after her first month here: *"I forgot to be afraid."*

### 4. The Golden Visa and a global base

Property valued at AED 2 million or more, roughly USD 545,000 or about R$3 million at recent rates, can qualify for the 10-year UAE Golden Visa for you and your family.

To be accurate: the Golden Visa lets you live in the UAE. It does not change where your Brazilian passport lets you travel. What it does is give you a long-term base in one of the most connected cities in the world.

### 5. A business platform

Brazil is a huge home market, but expanding abroad can be complex. In the UAE, foreign owners can hold 100% of a free zone company and of most mainland businesses. Corporate tax is 9% above AED 375,000 of profit, with 0% available on qualifying free zone income.

For Brazilian exporters, particularly in food, agribusiness and tech, a UAE entity can be a practical hub for the Gulf, Africa and South Asia.

## Brazilian tax: what to check before you buy

This is the section Brazilian investors should not skip.

- **Worldwide income.** Brazilian tax residents are generally taxed on worldwide income, including foreign rental income and capital gains.
- **Offshore rules.** Brazil tightened its rules on offshore investments, entities and trusts in 2023. Holding through a structure may not save tax and can create reporting obligations.
- **Reporting.** Foreign assets above certain thresholds must be declared to the Banco Central do Brasil, and property should be declared in your annual income tax return.
- **Leaving tax residency.** If you move to the UAE permanently, Brazil has a formal process to exit tax residency. Get advice on timing before you move.

The UAE side is simple: no personal income tax on rent. The Brazilian side is where the planning happens. Speak to a Brazilian tax adviser first.

## The practical part

1. **Getting there.** Emirates flies São Paulo to Dubai non-stop, roughly 14 to 15 hours. Brazilian passport holders have generally been able to get a visa on arrival, but check the ICP's current list before you fly.
2. **Moving money.** International transfers from Brazil to UAE developers are routine. Your bank or exchange provider will handle the câmbio paperwork. Keep every contract and receipt for your tax return.
3. **Fees.** Dubai's land department fee is 4%. Abu Dhabi's registration fee is around 2%. Budget for agency and admin costs too.
4. **Remote buying.** Most steps can be signed digitally. For ready property, a power of attorney lets someone complete the transfer for you.

## Where Brazilians tend to buy

- **Dubai Marina, JBR and Bluewaters.** The beach lifestyle Brazilians love, with strong holiday-let demand.
- **Palm Jumeirah.** The Copacabana of the Gulf, at a premium.
- **Business Bay and Downtown.** Rental demand from professionals and visitors.
- **Abu Dhabi's Saadiyat Island.** Culture and beach together: the Louvre, the Zayed National Museum, the Guggenheim opening December 2026, and a beach ranked #19 in the World's 50 Best Beaches 2026.

## The risks, said plainly

- **Currency mismatch.** If the real strengthens sharply, your dollar-linked returns are worth fewer reais. That cuts both ways.
- **Supply.** Dubai has a large pipeline of new homes due over the next few years. Location and quality decide which ones hold value.
- **Tax complexity.** Brazilian reporting rules are detailed. Get them right from day one.
- **Regional context.** Daily life in the UAE is very safe, but the wider region has its own geopolitics. Stay informed.

## My take

Brazilians already understand the two things that matter most in property: fundamentals and timing. What the UAE adds is stability. A currency fixed to the dollar. A city where rental income is not taxed locally. A ten-year residency route. A non-stop flight home.

You do not have to choose between Brazil and the UAE. The smartest families I know keep their roots in one and build a second base in the other.
