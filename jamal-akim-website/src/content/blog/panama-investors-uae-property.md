## The story: my family's road ran through Panama

I am Jamaican by way of Cuba and Panama. That is not a line I say for marketing. It is the route my family took.

In the 1850s, thousands of Jamaicans went to Panama to build the railroad across the isthmus. In the early 1900s, tens of thousands of West Indians, from Barbados, Jamaica, Saint Lucia and across the Caribbean, went back to dig the canal. Many stayed. Many moved on to Cuba to cut cane, and then on again. Those people followed the work, the infrastructure and the opportunity.

That is the same instinct I have now. I left London, moved to Dubai and built a career in property because the fundamentals were here.

So when a Panamanian investor asks me whether the UAE makes sense, I am not talking to a stranger. I am talking to the place my family passed through.

## The numbers: why the UAE fits a Panamanian portfolio

### No currency risk to speak of

This is the headline for Panama and it is unusual. Most of my Latin American clients carry some currency risk when they buy in the UAE, because their income is in reais, pesos or bolívares. A Panamanian investor earns, saves and spends in US dollars.

The dirham has held its peg to the dollar for nearly three decades. So for a Panamanian, buying in Dubai or Abu Dhabi is closer to buying in Miami than buying in Madrid. Same currency logic, different city.

That frees you to focus on what matters: the asset, the yield, the location and the developer.

The difference between a 3% city and a 6.5% city, on the same money, is more than USD 19,000 a year. Over a ten-year hold that is a serious number, before you count any capital growth.

Dubai's averages have run at roughly 6 to 7% gross in 2026. Some apartment communities do better, prime beachfront villas do less. Abu Dhabi is often similar, with a smaller and tighter market. Yields have been compressing as prices rise, so the unit you choose matters more than the city average.

### Capital growth driven by infrastructure

Panamanians know what a canal expansion does to land values. In the UAE, the equivalent projects are happening now:

- **Etihad Rail** began passenger service in 2026, linking the emirates.
- **Saadiyat Island** in Abu Dhabi has the Louvre, the Zayed National Museum (open since December 2025), the Guggenheim (opening December 2026) and a beach ranked #19 in the World's 50 Best Beaches 2026.
- **Disney** announced a theme park resort on Yas Island in 2025.
- **ADGM**, Abu Dhabi's financial centre, has drawn names like BlackRock and J.P. Morgan.

When a city keeps adding fundamentals like these, money follows. That is the logic of a hub, and it is the logic Panama was built on.

### Residency and a second base

Property valued at AED 2 million or more can qualify you and your family for the 10-year Golden Visa. It does not change your passport's travel rights, but it gives you a long-term base in a city that is one flight from most of Europe, Asia and Africa.

For Panamanian business owners in logistics, trade or services, that base matters. You can set up a UAE company with 100% foreign ownership in a free zone, open a bank account and serve markets that are hard to reach from Panama City in the same working day.

## Where Panamanian investors tend to start

Every brief is different, but these are the patterns I see.

- **Dubai Marina and JBR.** The closest thing to Punta Pacifica. Waterfront towers, strong short-let demand, high liquidity.
- **Business Bay and Downtown.** Like San Francisco and Obarrio: close to offices, strong rental demand from professionals.
- **Abu Dhabi's Saadiyat and Yas.** The long-term play. Culture, beaches, the Disney project and a tighter supply of villas.
- **Off-plan with a payment plan.** Spread the cost over construction. Some Abu Dhabi launches have offered as little as 5% down.

You can read my framework in [how I choose off-plan units](/blog/how-i-choose-off-plan-units) and see the market data in [Dubai's off-plan market, in real numbers](/blog/dubai-off-plan-market-in-numbers).

## The practical part

1. **Funds.** You can wire USD from a Panamanian bank. Ask your bank early what documents it needs for an international property payment; compliance teams can take longer than the developer.
2. **Fees.** Dubai's land department fee is 4% of the price. Abu Dhabi's registration fee is around 2%. Budget for agency, trustee and admin costs on top.
3. **Remote purchase.** You can sign most documents digitally. For the final transfer on ready property, a notarised power of attorney means you do not need to fly in.
4. **Ownership structure.** Some buyers purchase personally, some through a company. Get advice in both Panama and the UAE before you choose.

## The risks, said plainly

- **Distance.** There is no direct flight from Panama City at the time of writing, so plan on one connection.
- **Yield compression.** Rents have not kept pace with prices in every area. Pick the building, not the headline.
- **Supply.** Dubai is delivering a lot of new units in the next few years. Location and quality will decide which ones hold value.
- **Regional context.** The Gulf has its own geopolitics. Day to day, the UAE is among the safest places I have lived, but read the news and make your own call.

## My take

Panama taught the world that a small country can become essential by standing in the middle of everything. The UAE took the same idea and built it for the air age and the finance age.

For a Panamanian investor, the UAE is not a leap into the unknown. It is a familiar model with a dollar-linked currency, higher rental yields than most Western cities and a ten-year residency route.
