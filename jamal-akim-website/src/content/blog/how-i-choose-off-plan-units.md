Off-plan looks simple from a brochure. The real work happens long before a client sees a floor plan, and it never used to be as visible as it should be. Here's the actual process, not the pitch.

## Define the brief first

Before any project comes up, I map the investor's goals: rental yield vs. capital growth, exit timeline, Golden Visa eligibility, and their real budget ceiling once DLD and registration fees are included. Skipping this step is how people end up in the wrong unit for the right reasons.

## Vet the developer, not the brochure

Every launch looks impressive. The exceptional only becomes visible after handover. I look past the pitch for developers building something genuinely differentiated, design-led product, real material quality, and a track record of tenant and resale performance once the hype settles, not interchangeable stock competing purely on discount.

## Read the masterplan and the supply pipeline

With a large wave of units scheduled for handover over the next few years, I check the area's supply and demand density: what else is launching nearby, and whether that community can absorb the volume. A good unit in an oversaturated pipeline is still a risk.

## Run the numbers independently

Before anything reaches a shortlist, I pressure-test it against comparables and ask the questions a brochure or a single broker won't volunteer. The market is moving from impression to evidence, and clients should be deciding on proof, not a pitch.

## Underwrite the unit itself

Floor, orientation, layout efficiency, service charge load, and comparable rents in that specific building, not just the community average, decide whether a unit is actually a good deal.

## Stress-test the payment plan

I compare the real, escrow-backed payment schedule against list-price "discounts" that look generous but shift risk onto the buyer. Post-handover plans get modeled against actual cash flow, not the sales pitch.

## Legal and compliance, before signing

SPA terms, Oqood and DLD registration, and escrow account verification get confirmed before anyone signs anything, not after.

## Track it through to handover

I follow construction progress against the delivery schedule and flag any risk to the timeline well before completion, not the week of handover.

This is the process behind every recommendation I make. It's slower than sending a brochure, and that's the point.
