## The story: why two regions found each other

My family's story is a migration story. Jamaican by way of Cuba and Panama. Saint Lucian by way of Guyana. People from the Caribbean have always moved to where the work and the opportunity were. They built the Panama Canal. They cut cane in Cuba. They went where the fundamentals were.

That is exactly what I see happening now between Latin America and the Gulf.

Ten years ago, if a Brazilian family had money to protect, the playbook was simple: Miami, Lisbon, maybe Madrid. Today the conversation includes Dubai and Abu Dhabi, and it is not about luxury. It is about three things that Latin American investors understand better than almost anyone in the world:

1. **Currency.** If you have watched your savings lose half their value, you think differently about what a "stable" currency means.
2. **Safety.** If you have had to think about which car to drive or which route to take home, safety is not a lifestyle perk. It is the whole point.
3. **Optionality.** If your country's politics can change in one election, you want a second base. A plan B that is actually a plan A.

The UAE answers all three. That is the story. Now let's check it against the numbers.

## The numbers: five reasons the money is moving

### 1. The dirham is a dollar in a different suit

The UAE dirham has been pegged to the US dollar at **AED 3.6725 = USD 1** since 1997. Not a band. Not a managed float. A fixed rate, held for nearly three decades, through the 2008 crash, the 2014 oil collapse and the pandemic.

For a Latin American investor, that changes the risk picture completely. When you buy in Dubai, you are effectively buying a dollar-denominated asset, with rent paid in a dollar-linked currency, in a country with no personal income tax.

If you are from Panama or Ecuador, which already use the US dollar, there is no new currency risk at all. If you are from Brazil, Colombia or Mexico, your exposure is the same one you already hedge when you hold dollars.

Dubai's gross yields have been running at roughly **6 to 7%** on average in 2026. London and New York, the cities Latin American money has traditionally gone to, sit closer to **3%**.

Two honest notes, because this is where people get sold a dream:

- **Gross is not net.** Service charges, management, vacancy and furnishing usually take 1 to 2 points off.
- **Yields are compressing.** Prices have been rising faster than rents. That is good for owners who already bought, and it means new buyers need to choose carefully. Not every tower is a 7% tower.

I wrote more about how I filter projects in [how I choose off-plan units](/blog/how-i-choose-off-plan-units), and the raw sales data is in [Dubai's off-plan market, in real numbers](/blog/dubai-off-plan-market-in-numbers).

### 3. Safety you can measure

On Numbeo's 2026 safety index, Abu Dhabi ranked as the safest city in the world in the January edition, with Dubai in the global top ten. Mid-year, Ajman, Abu Dhabi and Dubai were all near the top of the table.

Crowdsourced indexes are not perfect. But when I talk to Latin American parents who have moved here, the measure they use is simpler: *my daughter takes a taxi home at midnight and I go to sleep.*

That sentence sells more property than any brochure. And to be balanced, the Gulf sits in a region with its own geopolitics, so any serious investor should read the current news and make their own call. In my experience, day-to-day life here is exceptionally safe.

### 4. Residency that lasts ten years

Property valued at **AED 2 million or more** (roughly USD 545,000) can qualify you for the UAE's **10-year Golden Visa**, which can include your spouse and children.

Smaller budgets are not locked out. The two-year property investor visa is also available, and its rules have been loosened in recent years.

One correction I make on almost every call: **a UAE Golden Visa does not give you visa-free travel to other countries.** Your passport decides that. What the UAE gives you is the base: a residency that does not depend on an employer, and an airport that connects you to most of the world in one flight.

### 5. A business base with no personal income tax

The UAE has no personal income tax. Corporate tax was introduced at **9%** on profits above AED 375,000, and qualifying free zone companies can still benefit from 0% on qualifying income. Foreigners can own 100% of companies in free zones and in most mainland activities.

For a Latin American entrepreneur, that means you can set up a holding or trading company in a time zone that overlaps with Asia, Europe and Africa in the same working day.

1. **Brief.** Budget, goal (yield, growth, residency or a home), timeline and which emirate.
2. **Shortlist.** Three to five units that fit, with honest numbers on service charges and comparable rents.
3. **Reserve.** A booking deposit, often 5 to 10% on off-plan.
4. **Contract and registration.** The sale and purchase agreement, then registration with the land department. In Dubai the transfer fee is **4%**; in Abu Dhabi registration is around **2%**.
5. **Payment plan.** Off-plan is paid in instalments during construction. Some plans run past handover. Aldar in Abu Dhabi has offered plans as light as 5% down on some launches.
6. **Handover and visa.** You receive the keys and title deed, then apply for residency if the property qualifies.

Money can be wired from your home bank in USD. Ask your bank early about the paperwork it needs to send international transfers, because in some Latin American countries that step takes longer than the purchase itself.

## Where the smart money is looking

I am based in Dubai, but I spend a lot of time on Abu Dhabi. My short version:

- **Dubai** for liquidity, rental demand and choice. The deepest resale market in the region.
- **Abu Dhabi** for long-term fundamentals. Saadiyat Island has the Louvre, the Zayed National Museum (open since December 2025) and the Guggenheim (opening December 2026), plus a beach ranked **#19 in the World's 50 Best Beaches 2026**. ADGM has brought global finance names like BlackRock and J.P. Morgan to the city.

Abu Dhabi is where I see a cultural district, a financial centre and a beach being built on the same island. Move those fundamentals to any other city and prices would follow. That is the logic I invest on.

## My take

I am not going to tell you the UAE is risk-free. Nothing is. Prices can soften, yields are compressing, and you need the right unit in the right building from the right developer.

But if you have grown up with currency crises, inflation or insecurity, the UAE offers an unusual combination: a dollar-linked currency, strong rental income, a ten-year residency route and some of the safest cities in the world. All in one place.

That is the bridge I am building. Latin America to the UAE. And the network on the other side keeps growing.
