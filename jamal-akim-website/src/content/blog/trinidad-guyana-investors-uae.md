When people ask where I am from, the honest answer takes a while.

British. Raised in London. Jamaican by way of Cuba and Panama. Saint Lucian by way of Guyana. Based in Dubai.

That last link, Saint Lucia by way of Guyana, is why this article matters to me. The southern Caribbean is part of my family's story. And right now, something historic is happening there. Guyana has become one of the fastest-growing economies on earth. Trinidad and Tobago has been an energy economy for a century. Both are producing a generation of investors who are asking the same question:

*Where do we build our second base?*

More and more of them are looking at the UAE.

## The story: oil changes everything, if you let it

I have spent years watching how the UAE handled its oil wealth. The short version: it treated oil as a starting point, not the destination.

Abu Dhabi saved through sovereign funds. It built Etihad, ADGM, Saadiyat Island's cultural district and now Etihad Rail. Dubai, with far less oil, built ports, an airline, tourism and a property market that people from 200 nationalities buy into. Today, by the UAE government's own figures, roughly three quarters of the economy comes from outside oil and gas.

Now look at Guyana.

Growth rates like these are almost unheard of. Oil production has passed 900,000 barrels a day. Guyana has a Natural Resource Fund. The question for Guyanese families and businesses is the one Abu Dhabi faced fifty years ago: how do you turn a resource boom into generational wealth?

Trinidad and Tobago knows the other side of that story. It has been an oil and gas producer for over a century and is one of the world's leading exporters of ammonia and methanol. It has seen booms and it has seen busts. Trinidadian investors understand cycles, and they understand why you do not keep everything in one place.

## The numbers: why the UAE fits Caribbean energy money

### A dollar-linked asset, outside your home currency

Both economies have currency pressure points. Trinidad and Tobago's dollar is managed against the US dollar, and businesses there have reported long-running difficulty getting hold of foreign exchange. Guyana's dollar has been steady, but a resource economy can change quickly when oil prices move.

The UAE dirham is pegged to the US dollar at **3.6725** and has been since 1997. Buying property in Dubai or Abu Dhabi gives you a hard-currency asset that pays rent in a dollar-linked currency, outside the energy cycle of your home country.

### Rental yields that pay you to wait

Dubai's average gross rental yields have run at roughly **6 to 7%** in 2026, well above London's typical 3%. For an investor whose main wealth is in an energy business or local property, that income stream is a useful counterweight.

The caveats I always give: gross is before service charges and management, and yields have been compressing as prices rise. The building you choose matters more than the city average. My filters are in [how I choose off-plan units](/blog/how-i-choose-off-plan-units).

### A city built for energy people

There is a practical point here too. The UAE is one of the world's major energy hubs. ADNOC, the global majors and the service companies all have a presence. Guyanese and Trinidadian engineers, executives and contractors often find their skills translate directly.

For energy professionals, the UAE can be more than an investment. It can be the next posting.

### Residency, tax and safety

- **Golden Visa.** Property valued at AED 2 million or more, roughly USD 545,000, can qualify for a 10-year residency for you and your family. It does not change your passport's travel rights; it gives you a base.
- **Tax.** No personal income tax in the UAE. Corporate tax is 9% above AED 375,000 of profit, with 0% possible on qualifying free zone income.
- **Safety.** In Numbeo's 2026 index, Abu Dhabi ranked first in the world in January, with Dubai in the top ten. For many Caribbean families, that alone is the reason.

## What Guyana can learn from Abu Dhabi

I am a property person, not a policymaker. But I think about this a lot, because it is the story of generational wealth at country level and at family level.

1. **Save the windfall.** Abu Dhabi built sovereign funds before it built skyscrapers. Families can do the same: put part of the boom into assets that pay you whether oil is at 50 or 100 dollars.
2. **Build fundamentals.** Saadiyat Island now has the Louvre Abu Dhabi, the Zayed National Museum and the Guggenheim opening in December 2026, plus a beach ranked #19 in the World's 50 Best Beaches 2026. Culture, education and infrastructure create value that outlasts any commodity.
3. **Invite the world in.** The UAE grew by welcoming talent and capital from everywhere. Openness is a growth strategy.
4. **Think in generations.** The decisions made in the 1970s still pay dividends today. The same is true for a family portfolio.

That is the logic I apply to property. Move Abu Dhabi's fundamentals to any other city, and money would follow. Guyana now has the chance to build its own.

## The practical part

1. **Travel.** No direct flights from Port of Spain or Georgetown at the time of writing. Most routes connect through North America, Europe or São Paulo. Check visa rules for your passport with the ICP before booking.
2. **Source of funds.** UAE developers and banks will ask how you earned the money. Contracts, company accounts, dividend records and bank statements make this simple.
3. **Transfers.** International wires in USD are routine. In Trinidad and Tobago, plan ahead for foreign exchange availability.
4. **Fees.** Dubai's land department fee is 4%. Abu Dhabi's registration fee is around 2%. Budget for agency and admin costs.
5. **Remote purchase.** Most documents can be signed digitally. A power of attorney can complete a ready property transfer without you flying in.

## Where Caribbean investors tend to start

- **Abu Dhabi: Saadiyat and Yas.** Long-term fundamentals, culture and beaches, with Disney planned on Yas.
- **Dubai Marina and JBR.** Waterfront living and strong short-let demand. Familiar to anyone who loves Maracas Bay.
- **Business Bay and Downtown.** Rental demand from professionals.
- **Off-plan with a payment plan.** Spread the cost across construction. Some Abu Dhabi launches have offered as little as 5% down.

## The risks, said plainly

- **Distance and travel time.** You will connect at least once.
- **Market cycles.** Prices have risen strongly. Buy for a mid to long hold, not a quick flip.
- **Supply.** Dubai has a large pipeline of new homes. Choose carefully.
- **Regional context.** Daily life in the UAE is very safe, but read the news on the wider region and make your own decision.
