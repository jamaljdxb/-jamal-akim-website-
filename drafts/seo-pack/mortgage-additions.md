Meta description: The Fed hiked in Sept 2026 and the UAE followed. What it means for EIBOR, Dubai mortgage rates, your monthly payment and your exit price, with the numbers.

### INSERT after "Let me show you."
## Key takeaways
- The Fed raised rates to 3.75% to 4.00% on 16 September 2026. The UAE Central Bank followed on 17 September, moving its Base Rate to 3.90%.
- 3-month EIBOR, the benchmark behind most UAE mortgages, rose from about 3.6% in April 2026 to 4.30% by 5 October 2026.
- Most buyers have not felt it yet. Most Dubai deals are cash, most borrowers are on fixed rates, and some banks still offer fixed rates below EIBOR.
- On a AED 1.6 million loan, 3.89% versus 5.30% is about AED 1,287 a month, and it cuts borrowing power by about 13%.
- The biggest impact is on exit prices at completion, when mortgage buyers become the main buyer pool.

### INSERT before "## Three ways the next year could go"
## Who feels a Fed hike the most: Dubai, London or New York?
Same decision in Washington. Three very different outcomes.

**New York.** Most American homeowners hold 30-year fixed mortgages. When rates rise, existing owners barely feel it. What they do instead is stop moving, because nobody wants to give up a cheap loan. Supply freezes and prices stay sticky.

**London.** The Bank of England sets its own rate. It watches the Fed but it is not tied to it. Most UK mortgages are two to five year fixes, so pain arrives in waves as those fixes end.

**Dubai and Abu Dhabi.** The UAE imports the Fed's decision directly through the dollar peg, usually within 24 hours. But because most buyers pay cash and most borrowers fix for a few years, the impact lands first on variable borrowers and on the budgets of new mortgage buyers.

| City | Rate follows the Fed? | Typical mortgage | Who feels a hike first |
|---|---|---|---|
| New York | Yes, it is the Fed | 30-year fixed | New buyers |
| London | No, Bank of England | 2 to 5 year fixed | Owners as fixes end |
| Dubai and Abu Dhabi | Yes, via the peg | 1 to 5 year fixed, then EIBOR | Variable borrowers and new buyers |

That is why I watch the Fed more closely than most London agents do. For us, it is not background noise. It is our interest rate.

### INSERT before "## Where to get your actual numbers"
## The jargon, in plain English
- **EIBOR.** The Emirates Interbank Offered Rate, published every business day for 1, 3 and 6 month periods. Most UAE variable mortgages are EIBOR plus a margin.
- **CBUAE Base Rate.** The rate on the Central Bank's overnight deposit facility. It is anchored to the Fed's interest on reserve balances.
- **Margin.** The fixed amount the bank adds on top of EIBOR. This is where banks compete.
- **Reversion rate.** What your mortgage switches to when the fixed period ends, usually EIBOR plus a margin.
- **Loan-to-value (LTV).** The loan as a share of the property's value.
- **Debt burden ratio.** Your total monthly debt payments as a share of income. Capped at 50% in the UAE.

## ARTICLE 2: non-resident-mortgage-dubai-2026
Meta title: Non-Resident Mortgage Dubai 2026: Deposit, Rates, Rules
Meta description: Can you get a Dubai mortgage without UAE residency? Yes. Deposit, loan-to-value, income, age, documents, fees and rates for non-resident buyers in 2026.

### INSERT after "So let me give it to you straight."
## Key takeaways
- Non-residents can get UAE mortgages. Most banks lend 50% to 60% of the value of a completed property, and some go to 65%.
- Budget a 40% to 50% deposit, plus roughly 5% to 7% in fees.
- The 50% debt cap counts every debt you have worldwide, not just in the UAE.
- Most off-plan buyers use the developer payment plan, then take a handover mortgage at completion.
- Pre-approval through GCC Mortgages typically takes 24 hours once your documents are in.

### INSERT before "## Rates for non-residents right now"
## Non-resident buyers by country: what changes
The bank's rules are the same for everyone. What changes is the paperwork, the money transfer and the tax at home. Always check the home side with a qualified adviser.

**United Kingdom.** UK credit reports are widely accepted by UAE banks. UK residents are generally taxed on worldwide income, so rent from a Dubai flat usually goes on your UK tax return.

**United States.** US citizens are taxed on worldwide income wherever they live, and foreign bank accounts above set thresholds must be reported. Expect extra paperwork from UAE banks for US persons. It is doable, but start early.

**India.** Remittances for property abroad usually go through the Liberalised Remittance Scheme, which has an annual limit per person, and tax collected at source can apply above certain thresholds. Plan the timing of your payments around those rules.

**Nigeria.** The main challenge is moving funds and proving where they came from. Many buyers pay from domiciliary USD accounts held in Nigeria or abroad. Keep every statement.

**Canada.** Canadian residents are taxed on worldwide income, and foreign property above set thresholds must be reported each year.

**Latin America.** For Brazil, Panama, Venezuela and the Caribbean, see my [Latin America guides](/latin-america/). The dollar peg is often the main reason buyers choose the UAE.

### INSERT before "## Currency, transfers and rent"
## How long does it take?
From first call to keys, a non-resident purchase of a ready property usually runs four to eight weeks.
- **Pre-approval:** about 24 hours once your documents are complete.
- **Offer and MOU:** a few days of negotiation, then a 10% deposit cheque to secure the deal.
- **Valuation and final offer:** around one to two weeks.
- **Transfer:** booked at a trustee office once the bank, buyer and seller are ready. A power of attorney means you do not have to fly in.
The slowest part is almost never Dubai. It is your home bank releasing the funds and the paperwork proving where they came from. Start that on day one.

## ARTICLE 3: resident-mortgage-uae-2026
Meta title: UAE Resident Mortgage 2026: How Much Can You Borrow?
Meta description: Expat resident mortgages in the UAE: 80% loan-to-value, the 50% debt cap, fixed vs variable after the Fed hike, salary transfer, Islamic finance, fees and rent vs buy.

### INSERT after "Here is how to tell the difference."
## Key takeaways
- Expat residents can borrow up to 80% on a first home up to AED 5 million. Off-plan is capped at 50%.
- The 50% debt burden cap usually decides your limit, not the bank's minimum salary.
- If you will stay four to five years or more, buying usually beats renting, even with zero price growth.
- Fixed offers around 3.75% to 3.89% are still below variable pricing after the September 2026 hike.
- When your fixed period ends, compare banks. Switching can save around 1% a year.

### INSERT before "## Documents for residents"
## Self-employed? Here is what changes
Business owners get mortgages here every day. The bank just needs more proof.
- **Trading history.** Most banks want at least two years of trading under your licence.
- **Accounts.** Two years of audited financial statements, plus six to twelve months of business and personal bank statements.
- **Income.** Banks usually average your income over two years and may count only part of it. Pay yourself a consistent salary in the run-up.
- **Terms.** Some banks offer slightly lower loan-to-value or slightly higher rates to self-employed borrowers. A broker earns their fee here by knowing which banks like your profile.

## First-time buyer checklist
1. Check your debt burden: list every loan and card limit.
2. Close the credit cards you do not use.
3. Get a pre-approval before you view properties.
4. Decide how long you will really stay.
5. Set a budget that includes 5% to 7% in fees.
6. Compare fixed and variable, including reversion margins.
7. Ask whether a salary transfer earns a better rate.
8. Check the service charge history of the building.
9. Get the property valued before you commit to the price.
10. Diary your fixed-rate end date the day you sign.

### INSERT before "## Insurance you will be asked for"
## Can a mortgaged home get me the Golden Visa?
Often, yes. Property valued at AED 2 million or more can qualify for the 10-year Golden Visa, and mortgaged property can count. The exact rules on how much must already be paid and what letter the bank must provide have changed over time, so confirm the current criteria with the Dubai Land Department or ICP before you buy for the visa.
For many residents, the real benefit is independence. Your residency no longer depends on your employer. If you change jobs, start a business or take a break, your visa stays. That alone changes how a lot of families think about buying.

## ARTICLE 4: off-plan-vs-resale-day-one-cash
Meta title: Developer vs Resale Dubai: Why Cheaper Costs AED 2M More
Meta description: Why would anyone pay the developer AED 1.35m more than resale? Day-one cash, leverage and the four phases of price appreciation, with Abu Dhabi and Dubai examples.

### INSERT after "Let me show you, with a real-world style example from Abu Dhabi."
## Key takeaways
- Resale before completion means refunding what the seller has already paid, plus a premium, fees and agency.
- In our Abu Dhabi example, resale needs about AED 3.15 million on day one. Developer direct needs about AED 1.1 million.
- Before completion you sell to investors, who negotiate hard. At completion, mortgage buyers and end users arrive.
- Developers give you time. Mortgages give end users time. Investors want cash today.
- Run the day-one cash maths on every deal. The sticker price lies.

### INSERT before "## When resale does make sense"
## What happens at completion: the exit, worked through
Back to our Abu Dhabi unit bought direct at AED 9.25 million.

By handover you have paid 10% on booking and, say, another 30% during construction. That is AED 3.7 million plus the AED 185,000 registration fee, so about **AED 3.885 million in**. The final 60%, AED 5.55 million, is due at handover.

Now you have three choices.

**1. Sell at completion.** If the market values the unit at AED 9.5 million, a mortgage buyer can pay that with a deposit and a bank loan. The developer balance is cleared from the sale. You walk away with roughly AED 250,000 above your price before agency fees, on about AED 3.9 million of cash. That is the illustrative upside. If completion value comes in at AED 9 million, you are selling at a loss. That is the risk.

**2. Hold with a handover mortgage.** Investment property loans for expats often run at around 60% of value. Sixty percent of AED 9.25 million is AED 5.55 million, which happens to match the balance. The catch is that the bank lends on its own valuation, not your price. If it values the unit lower, you fund the gap.

**3. Pay cash and hold.** No interest, full rent, and maximum exposure to one asset.

[CHART: Waterfall of the developer-direct journey: AED 1.11m on day one, AED 2.775m during construction, AED 5.55m at handover, funded by sale, mortgage or cash.]

The lesson is simple. The day-one saving is real, but your plan for the 60% decides whether the deal works.

## The jargon, in plain English
- **Payment plan.** Instalments to the developer during construction, usually interest free.
- **Post-handover plan.** Part of the price is paid after you get the keys.
- **SPA.** The sale and purchase agreement.
- **Oqood.** Dubai's off-plan registration with the land department.
- **NOC.** The developer's no objection certificate, needed to resell before completion, often with a fee.
- **Premium.** What a resale buyer pays the seller above the original price.
