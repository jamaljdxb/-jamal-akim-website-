## PANAMA: INSERT before "## The practical part"
## Questions Panamanian investors ask me
**Which emirate should I start with?** Dubai if you want liquidity and the deepest rental market. Abu Dhabi if you want long-term fundamentals and less supply. Ras Al Khaimah if you want an early-stage story around the Wynn resort. Most of my Panamanian clients start in Dubai and add Abu Dhabi later.
**Short let or long let?** Short lets, holiday homes, need a permit from Dubai's Department of Economy and Tourism and an operator, and income moves with the seasons. Long lets are registered through Ejari and give steadier, lower-maintenance income. If you are managing from Panama City, long let is usually the calmer choice.
**Can I manage it from Panama?** Yes. A good property manager handles tenants, maintenance and service charges for a percentage of rent. You sign most documents digitally and use a power of attorney for anything that needs a signature in person.
**What about insurance?** Buildings carry their own structural insurance through the service charge. Take your own contents and landlord cover, which is inexpensive in the UAE.
**Who will I sell to later?** That depends on when you sell. Before completion, other investors. After completion, mortgage buyers and end users, which is usually where the fairer price is. Plan your exit around that moment.

## Work and business: what Venezuelans actually do here
The UAE does not need you to start from zero. It needs your skills.
**Energy.** Venezuela trained generations of petroleum engineers, geologists and project managers. The UAE is one of the world's great energy hubs, and that experience transfers directly.

The theme is the same every time. Bring the resilience you already have, add the right paperwork, and the UAE rewards it.

## BRAZIL: INSERT before "## Where Brazilians tend to buy"
## Short let or long let? Running a Dubai property from Brazil
This is the decision that shapes your returns more than almost anything else.
**Long let.** A tenant signs a one-year contract, registered through Ejari. Rent is often paid in a small number of cheques. Income is steady, management is light and vacancy is low in good areas. Rent increases follow Dubai's official rental index, so you cannot simply raise the rent to any number.
**Short let.** Holiday homes need a permit from Dubai's Department of Economy and Tourism and usually a licensed operator, who typically takes a meaningful share of revenue. Income can be higher in peak months and lower in summer. You also pay for furnishing, cleaning and utilities.
**My rule for Brazilian owners.** If you will rarely be in Dubai, start with a long let. It is simpler to manage from São Paulo and easier to explain to the Receita Federal. If you want to use the apartment yourself for a few weeks a year, a short let can work, but run the numbers net of the operator's share and the quiet summer months.
**Either way, think about the exit.** If you bought off-plan, your best buyer often arrives at completion, when mortgage buyers and end users can finally purchase. A well-let, well-kept unit sells faster to both.

## TRINIDAD AND GUYANA: INSERT before "## The practical part"
## Questions Caribbean investors ask me
**Can I buy through a company?** Often, yes, but it changes the paperwork, the bank's view and possibly your tax at home. Many buyers keep it simple and buy personally. Get advice in your home country before deciding.
**Can family members co-own?** Yes. Joint ownership is common. Agree in writing who pays what and what happens if one owner wants to sell.
**What happens if I die?** This is the question most buyers skip. Without a registered will, UAE succession can follow rules you did not intend. Non-Muslims can register wills through the DIFC Courts Wills Service in Dubai or the Abu Dhabi Judicial Department. It is one of the smartest small costs in the whole purchase.
**Who manages it while I am in Port of Spain or Georgetown?** A licensed property manager, paid a percentage of rent, handles tenants, maintenance and service charges. Choose one with clear monthly statements.
**When should I sell?** If you bought off-plan, completion is usually the moment the buyer pool widens, because mortgage buyers and end users arrive. If you are holding for income, the right answer may be never. That is how Abu Dhabi thought about its assets, and it is how generational wealth is built.

## Why the peg matters more than the yield
Most people read an article like this and fixate on the 6 or 7%. I understand why. It is the number that jumps off the page.
But for a Panamanian investor, the quiet advantage is the peg. Every other international buyer I work with has to ask two questions: will the property do well, and will my currency hold up against the dirham? You only have to ask the first one.
That is rare. A Brazilian buyer can be right about the property and still lose money in reais. A British buyer can be right and lose it in pounds. A Panamanian buyer who picks the right building keeps the full return, in dollars, from day one to the day they sell.
Yield gets you interested. The peg is what lets you sleep.

For families, schools usually decide the area before anything else.
Dubai's private schools are inspected and rated by the KHDA, the emirate's education regulator, and the ratings are public. ## BRAZIL: INSERT before "## My take"
## The Golden Visa, explained for Brazilian families
The 10-year Golden Visa is the reason many Brazilian families look at the AED 2 million mark.
Here is what it does, in plain terms. It lets you live in the UAE without an employer sponsoring you. It can usually include your spouse and children, and in many cases your parents. It is designed to stay valid even if you spend long periods outside the UAE, which suits families who split their year between Brazil and Dubai.
Here is what it does not do. It does not give you UAE citizenship, and it does not change where your Brazilian passport lets you travel. It also has conditions, including property valuation and, for mortgaged homes, rules about what has been paid.
Confirm the current requirements with the ICP or the Dubai Land Department before you buy for the visa. The rules have changed before and will change again.

## TRINIDAD AND GUYANA: INSERT before "## My take"
## What Trinidad's cycles can teach a Guyanese investor
Trinidad and Tobago has lived through more than one energy boom and more than one bust. When prices were high, money flowed into local property, cars and imports. When prices fell, foreign exchange became scarce and the people who had kept everything at home felt it most.
Guyana is at the start of its own story, with growth rates the world has rarely seen. The lesson from Port of Spain is not to fear the boom. It is to diversify during it.
That can mean a business at home and a property abroad. Local assets for growth, and a dollar-linked asset that pays rent whatever oil does. Abu Dhabi did exactly that at national scale, and it is why its economy no longer rises and falls with the oil price alone.
The best time to build a second base is while the first one is booming.
