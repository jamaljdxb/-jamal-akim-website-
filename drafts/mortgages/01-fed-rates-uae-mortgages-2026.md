Look, I get this question every week now.

*"Jamal, the Fed raised rates. Does that even matter here?"*

Short answer: yes. Long answer: not as much as the headlines say, not yet, and not for everyone. But it matters a lot to one group of people. And if you are buying off-plan, that group is the person you will eventually sell to.

Let me show you.

## Key takeaways
- The Fed raised rates to 3.75% to 4.00% on 16 September 2026. The UAE Central Bank followed on 17 September, moving its Base Rate to 3.90%.
- 3-month EIBOR, the benchmark behind most UAE mortgages, rose from about 3.6% in April 2026 to 4.30% by 5 October 2026.
- Most buyers have not felt it yet. Most Dubai deals are cash, most borrowers are on fixed rates, and some banks still offer fixed rates below EIBOR.
- On a AED 1.6 million loan, 3.89% versus 5.30% is about AED 1,287 a month, and it cuts borrowing power by about 13%.
- The biggest impact is on exit prices at completion, when mortgage buyers become the main buyer pool.


## The pipe from Washington to Dubai

The dirham is pegged to the US dollar at **3.6725**. It has been since 1997. To keep that peg, the UAE cannot run its own interest rate policy. When the Fed moves, the Central Bank of the UAE moves with it, usually within a day.

So the pipe looks like this:

1. **The Fed** sets the US rate.
2. **The CBUAE** moves its Base Rate to match the Fed's interest on reserve balances.
3. **EIBOR**, the rate banks lend to each other at in dirhams, follows.
4. **Your mortgage** is priced as EIBOR plus a margin, or as a fixed rate the bank sets with EIBOR in mind.

Here is what actually happened.

| Date | Fed target range | CBUAE Base Rate |
|---|---|---|
| July 2023 (peak) | 5.25% to 5.50% | 5.40% |
| December 2024 | 4.25% to 4.50% | 4.40% |
| December 2025 | 3.50% to 3.75% | 3.65% |
| 17 September 2026 | 3.75% to 4.00% | **3.90%** |

Series 2: CBUAE Base Rate (5.40, 4.90, 4.40, 4.15, 3.90, 3.65, flat, 3.90). The Fed cut three times in late 2024 and three more in late 2025. Then in September 2026 it reversed and **hiked by 25 basis points**, its first increase since July 2023. The CBUAE raised the Base Rate from 3.65% to 3.90% the next morning. Markets were pricing at least one more hike at the time.

## So have we felt it?

Here is my honest read: **mostly not yet.** And there are four reasons why.

### 1. Most of Dubai buys with cash

Depending on the source and the month, somewhere between two thirds and 86% of Dubai transactions are cash. Knight Frank put cash at about 86% of volume in the first three quarters of 2025. Other trackers put mortgage share closer to a third in early 2026. Either way, the majority of buyers do not care what EIBOR does on a Tuesday.

### 2. Most borrowers are fixed

The typical UAE mortgage is fixed for one to five years, then reverts to EIBOR plus a margin. If you fixed in 2025, the hike does not touch you until your fix ends. That is a delayed fuse, not no fuse.

### 3. Banks are still fighting for business

This is the strange one. On the 5 October fixing, 3-month EIBOR was **4.30%**. Yet some of the big banks were still advertising fixed rates from around **3.75% to 3.89%** for salary transfer customers. That is a fixed rate below the floating benchmark. Banks do that to win market share. It does not last forever.

### 4. The market moved before the Fed did

3-month EIBOR was around 3.6% in April 2026. By early October it was 4.30%. That is roughly 70 basis points of rise, most of it before the Fed even announced. Variable-rate borrowers have already been paying more for months. They just did not get a headline.

> The hike has landed on variable borrowers. It is waiting for fixed borrowers. And it has barely touched cash buyers.

## What it does to a real monthly payment

Let me make it real. AED 2 million apartment. Resident buyer. 80% loan, so AED 1.6 million borrowed over 25 years.

| Rate | Monthly payment | Per year |
|---|---|---|
| 3.89% (today's best fixed offers) | AED 8,349 | AED 100,188 |
| 4.39% | AED 8,794 | AED 105,528 |
| 4.89% | AED 9,251 | AED 111,012 |
| 5.30% (3M EIBOR 4.30% + 1.00% margin) | AED 9,635 | AED 115,620 |

[CHART: Bar chart of the monthly payment at each rate, highlighting the gap between 3.89% and 5.30%: about AED 1,287 a month, or AED 15,440 a year.]

The difference between a good fixed deal today and a variable rate at today's EIBOR is about **AED 1,287 a month**. Over a year that is more than AED 15,000.

Now flip it around. If a buyer can afford AED 8,349 a month and the rate goes from 3.89% to 5.30%, what they can borrow falls from AED 1.6 million to about **AED 1.39 million**. That is a **13% cut in buying power** from one move in rates.

That number is the one I want you to remember. Not because of your mortgage. Because of the next buyer's.

## Why this matters even if you pay cash

Here is the part nobody explains properly.

A building goes through four phases, and each phase has a different buyer.

[GRAPHIC: The 4 phases of price appreciation. Off-plan: investors. Construction: investors. Completion: leveraged buyers. Maturity: end users.]

- **Off-plan and construction.** Your buyer is another investor. They want margin. Your profit is literally their discount, so they squeeze.
- **Completion.** The building is real. Banks will now lend on it. A whole new pool arrives: **leveraged buyers**, people using mortgages.
- **Maturity.** **End users** who want to live there, often also with a mortgage, pay for the home, not the spreadsheet.

So who sets your exit price when you sell at completion? The mortgage buyer. And what decides how much the mortgage buyer can pay? **The rate.**

If rates drift up another point between now and your handover in 2028, your completion buyer has less to spend. If rates come back down, they have more. That is how a decision in Washington ends up in your profit on a tower in Business Bay.

## Who feels a Fed hike the most: Dubai, London or New York?
Same decision in Washington. Three very different outcomes.

**New York.** Most American homeowners hold 30-year fixed mortgages. When rates rise, existing owners barely feel it. What they do instead is stop moving, because nobody wants to give up a cheap loan. Supply freezes and prices stay sticky.

**London.** The Bank of England sets its own rate. It watches the Fed but it is not tied to it. Most UK mortgages are two to five year fixes, so pain arrives in waves as those fixes end.

**Dubai and Abu Dhabi.** The UAE imports the Fed's decision directly through the dollar peg, usually within 24 hours. But because most buyers pay cash and most borrowers fix for a few years, the impact lands first on variable borrowers and on the budgets of new mortgage buyers.

| City | Rate follows the Fed? | Typical mortgage | Who feels a hike first |
|---|---|---|---|
| New York | Yes, it is the Fed | 30-year fixed | New buyers |
| London | No, Bank of England | 2 to 5 year fixed | Owners as fixes end |
| Dubai and Abu Dhabi | Yes, via the peg | 1 to 5 year fixed, then EIBOR | Variable borrowers and new buyers |

That is why I watch the Fed more closely than most London agents do. For us, it is not background noise. It is our interest rate.

## Three ways the next year could go

Nobody knows where rates go next, including the Fed. So I plan with scenarios, not predictions.

**Scenario one: more hikes.** When the Fed moved in September 2026, markets were pricing another quarter point before the end of the year and more into 2027. If that happens, 3-month EIBOR likely pushes towards 4.5% to 4.75%. Fixed offers below 4% disappear. Variable borrowers feel it every month. Completion buyers in 2027 and 2028 can borrow less.

**Scenario two: a long hold.** The Fed sits at 3.75% to 4% for a year. EIBOR settles around today's level. Banks keep competing on fixed rates, and the market gets used to it. This is the boring scenario, and boring is fine for property.

**Scenario three: back to cuts.** If the US economy slows, the Fed reverses again. EIBOR falls, variable payments drop and borrowing power returns. This is the scenario that helps an off-plan seller at completion the most.

[CHART: Simple three-line fan chart of 3-month EIBOR into 2027: hikes (towards 4.75%), hold (around 4.3%), cuts (towards 3.5%). Labelled "illustrative scenarios, not a forecast".]

You do not need to pick one. You need a plan that survives all three. That means a payment you can afford in scenario one, and an exit price that does not depend on scenario three.

## What I would do right now

**If you are buying with a mortgage in the next six months:**
- Get a pre-approval now, while fixed offers are still competitive.
- Compare the reversion margin, not just the headline fixed rate. A 3.89% fix that reverts to EIBOR plus 1.99% is a very different loan from one that reverts at plus 1.00%.
- Check the early settlement terms. If you might sell or refinance in three years, they matter.

**If you are already on a fixed rate:**
- Put your fix end date in your calendar now, and start comparing banks three months before it.

**If you are buying off-plan with cash:**
- Stress test your exit. Ask: if rates are one point higher at handover, what can my completion buyer afford? Price your expectations from that.

## The jargon, in plain English
- **EIBOR.** The Emirates Interbank Offered Rate, published every business day for 1, 3 and 6 month periods. Most UAE variable mortgages are EIBOR plus a margin.
- **CBUAE Base Rate.** The rate on the Central Bank's overnight deposit facility. It is anchored to the Fed's interest on reserve balances.
- **Margin.** The fixed amount the bank adds on top of EIBOR. This is where banks compete.
- **Reversion rate.** What your mortgage switches to when the fixed period ends, usually EIBOR plus a margin.
- **Loan-to-value (LTV).** The loan as a share of the property's value.
- **Debt burden ratio.** Your total monthly debt payments as a share of income. Capped at 50% in the UAE.
