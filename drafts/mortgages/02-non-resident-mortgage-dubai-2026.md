Here is the conversation I have most with buyers in London, Lagos, Toronto and São Paulo.

*"Jamal, I don't live in Dubai. Can I even get a mortgage there?"*

Yes. You can. But the rules are different from the ones residents get, and most of the articles online either oversell it or bury you in jargon. So let me give it to you straight.

## The headline numbers

| | Non-resident (typical) | Resident expat, first home |
|---|---|---|
| Loan-to-value, completed property | 50% to 60% (some lenders up to 65%) | Up to 80% (up to AED 5m) |
| Deposit you bring | 40% to 50% plus fees | 20% plus fees |
| Off-plan | Limited, usually up to 50% near handover | Up to 50% |
| Maximum term | 15 to 25 years | Up to 25 years |
| Age at final payment | 65 salaried, 70 self-employed | 65 salaried, 70 self-employed |
| Debt burden cap | 50% of income, all debts worldwide | 50% of income |

[CHART: Two stacked bars for an AED 2m apartment. Non-resident at 60% LTV: AED 800k deposit + about AED 90k fees, AED 1.2m loan. Resident at 80% LTV: AED 400k deposit + about AED 90k fees, AED 1.6m loan.]

These are lender practices, not one fixed law. The Central Bank sets ceilings, and each bank decides how close to the ceiling it will go for your profile. Your nationality, income type, credit history and the property all move the number.

## What "non-resident" actually means

If you do not hold a UAE residence visa, you are a non-resident to the bank. It does not matter how often you visit. It does not matter if you own a company here. No Emirates ID, no resident pricing.

That is why the order matters. Some of my clients buy a property that qualifies them for a UAE residence visa, get the visa, and then refinance later as residents on better terms. Others are happy to stay non-resident. Both work. Plan it before you buy.

## The four things the bank looks at

### 1. Income

Most banks want to see a minimum income of around **AED 15,000 to 25,000 a month**, or the equivalent in your home currency. Salaried is easiest. Self-employed is very doable, but expect to show two years of company accounts and personal bank statements.

### 2. Your debt burden

This is the one that catches people. The UAE caps your total monthly debt repayments at **50% of your gross monthly income**. And for non-residents, that means **everything, worldwide**. Your mortgage in Manchester. Your car finance in Houston. Your credit cards. They all count.

So if you earn the equivalent of AED 40,000 a month and already pay AED 12,000 on a home loan back home, your maximum new Dubai payment is AED 8,000, not AED 20,000.

### 3. Credit history

Bring a credit report from your home country, usually less than three months old. A clean report is the single biggest thing you can do to speed up approval.

### 4. The property

Banks lend on completed property far more readily than off-plan. They also run their own valuation. If the bank values the unit below your purchase price, they lend on the lower number and you fund the gap.

## Off-plan: how non-residents actually do it

Most non-residents I work with who want off-plan do not use a mortgage during construction at all. They use the **developer's payment plan**, which is interest free, and pay 10% to 20% upfront and the rest in stages.

Then at handover, when the building is complete and banks will lend on it, they either:

- pay the final balance in cash, or
- take a **handover mortgage** to cover what is left.

## Documents to have ready

- Passport (and visa page if you have one anywhere)
- Six months of personal bank statements
- Salary certificate or employment contract and payslips, if salaried
- Two years of audited company accounts and company statements, if self-employed
- Home-country credit report, under three months old
- Proof of address
- The property details: reservation form or MOU, and the developer statement for off-plan

1. **Pre-approval.** A broker compares banks and gets you an approval in principle. GCC Mortgages typically issues this within 24 hours once they have your documents.
2. **Find the property.** With a pre-approval, you negotiate like a cash buyer.
3. **Valuation.** The bank values the property.
4. **Final offer.** The bank issues the formal offer letter.
5. **Transfer.** At the trustee office, the property transfers, the mortgage is registered with the DLD and the keys are yours. You can sign through a power of attorney if you cannot fly in.

## Currency, transfers and rent

Two things non-residents forget until it is too late.

**The exchange rate.** Your loan is in dirhams, and the dirham is pegged to the US dollar. If you earn in pounds, euros, naira or rupees, your real monthly cost moves with your currency against the dollar. A 10% fall in your home currency is a 10% rise in your mortgage, in your money. Some buyers hold a buffer in dollars or dirhams for exactly this reason. Use a regulated FX provider for the deposit too. On a AED 800,000 transfer, a bad bank rate can quietly cost you tens of thousands.

**The rent covers more than you think, but not everything.** If the unit rents for AED 120,000 a year, that is AED 10,000 a month against a AED 7,226 mortgage payment. Good. But take off the service charge, management, a month of vacancy and maintenance, and the surplus shrinks fast. Banks also do not count future rent from the property you are buying as income when they assess you, so you must qualify on what you earn today.

## What about the Golden Visa?

Property valued at **AED 2 million or more** can qualify you for the 10-year Golden Visa. Mortgaged property can count, but the rules on how much must be paid and what the bank needs to confirm change, so confirm the current criteria with the DLD or ICP before you rely on it. And remember: the Golden Visa lets you live here. It does not change where your passport lets you travel.

## Mistakes I see non-residents make

- **Forgetting the home loan.** The 50% debt cap includes debts outside the UAE.
- **Not budgeting fees.** The deposit is not the cheque. Fees add 4% to 7% on top.
- **Assuming off-plan finance.** Most off-plan is cash or payment plan until handover.
- **Comparing only headline rates.** The reversion margin and early settlement terms often matter more.
- **Shopping one bank.** Rates and appetite for non-residents vary a lot. Compare.
