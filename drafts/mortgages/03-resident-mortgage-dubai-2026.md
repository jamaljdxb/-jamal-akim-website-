If you live here, you have probably done this maths on a napkin.

*"I'm paying 140K a year in rent. My landlord's mortgage is probably half that. What am I doing?"*

Fair question. Sometimes buying is the smartest move you will make in the UAE. Sometimes it is a trap. Here is how to tell the difference.

## Key takeaways
- Expat residents can borrow up to 80% on a first home up to AED 5 million. Off-plan is capped at 50%.
- The 50% debt burden cap usually decides your limit, not the bank's minimum salary.
- If you will stay four to five years or more, buying usually beats renting, even with zero price growth.
- Fixed offers around 3.75% to 3.89% are still below variable pricing after the September 2026 hike.
- When your fixed period ends, compare banks. Switching can save around 1% a year.


## What residents can borrow

The Central Bank sets the ceilings. Banks decide how close to go.

| Buyer | Property | Maximum loan-to-value |
|---|---|---|
| Expat resident | First home, up to AED 5m | 80% |
| Expat resident | First home, above AED 5m | 70% |
| UAE national | First home, up to AED 5m | 85% |
| UAE national | First home, above AED 5m | 75% |
| Expat resident | Second or investment property | around 60% |
| Anyone | Off-plan | 50% |

Some banks quote slightly lower ceilings. Treat these as the top of the range, not a promise.

## The rule that really decides it: 50%

Your total monthly debt repayments cannot be more than **50% of your gross monthly income**. That includes your new mortgage, car loan, personal loans and credit card limits, which banks usually count at around 5% of the limit each month even if you clear them.

At today's rates, here is what a AED 1.6 million loan over 25 years needs, assuming no other debts:

| Rate | Monthly payment | Income needed at 50% |
|---|---|---|
| 3.89% fixed | AED 8,349 | about AED 16,700 |
| 5.30% variable | AED 9,635 | about AED 19,300 |

That gap is the Fed hike showing up in your life. I explain the whole chain in [the Fed and Dubai mortgages](/insights/fed-rates-uae-mortgages-2026/).

Tip: cancel the credit cards you do not use before you apply. A AED 50,000 limit you never touch can cost you real borrowing power.

## Rent vs buy, with real numbers

Let me take a common one. AED 2 million two-bed. Rent on the same unit: AED 130,000 a year.

**Buying with 80% finance:**
- Deposit: AED 400,000
- Fees (DLD 4%, mortgage registration, valuation, arrangement, trustee): about AED 95,000 to 115,000
- Mortgage at 3.89% over 25 years: AED 8,349 a month, about AED 100,000 a year
- Service charge: say AED 25,000 a year
- Maintenance and insurance: say AED 8,000 a year

That is around **AED 133,000 a year** going out, against AED 130,000 rent. Almost identical.

But here is the difference. Part of every mortgage payment pays down your loan. In year one, roughly AED 38,000 of that AED 100,000 is principal. That is money you keep. Rent is gone.

[CHART: Two lines over 10 years. Cumulative rent paid vs cumulative cost of owning minus equity built (principal repaid only, no price growth assumed). Ownership crosses below renting around year 4 to 5.]

So the rule I give clients:

- **Staying four to five years or more?** Buying usually wins, even with zero price growth.
- **Might leave in two to three years?** The 4% transfer fee and selling costs usually make renting cheaper.
- **Unsure about your job or visa?** Rent until you are sure.

## Fixed or variable after the hike

Most UAE mortgages are fixed for one to five years, then revert to EIBOR plus a margin.

**Fixed makes sense if:**
- you want to know your payment to the dirham,
- you think rates may rise further (markets were pricing more hikes when the Fed moved in September 2026),
- the fixed offer is close to or below variable pricing, which it is right now at several banks.

**Variable makes sense if:**
- you expect to pay down the loan fast or sell soon,
- you believe rates will fall, and you can absorb the payment going up if you are wrong.

Whatever you pick, read three lines in the offer letter: the **reversion margin**, the **early settlement fee**, and whether there is a **minimum rate floor** on the variable part.

## Salary transfer or not

Many UAE banks give their best rates only if you move your salary to them. That can be worth 0.25% to 0.5% a year. If your employer is on the bank's approved list, it is usually worth doing. If not, a broker can find banks that price well without it.

## Islamic home finance

You do not have to take an interest-based loan. Most UAE banks offer Islamic home finance through structures like **ijara** (the bank buys and leases to you) or **murabaha** (the bank buys and sells to you at an agreed profit). Pricing is benchmarked in a similar way, and the protections on early settlement apply. If it matters to you, ask for it. GCC Mortgages compares conventional and Islamic options side by side.

## Buying off-plan as a resident

Banks cap off-plan finance at 50%, and many will only lend near completion. Most residents use the developer payment plan during construction, then take a **handover mortgage** for the balance.

## Can a mortgaged home get me the Golden Visa?
Often, yes. Property valued at AED 2 million or more can qualify for the 10-year Golden Visa, and mortgaged property can count. The exact rules on how much must already be paid and what letter the bank must provide have changed over time, so confirm the current criteria with the Dubai Land Department or ICP before you buy for the visa.
For many residents, the real benefit is independence. Your residency no longer depends on your employer. If you change jobs, start a business or take a break, your visa stays. That alone changes how a lot of families think about buying.

## Insurance you will be asked for

Every UAE bank will make two policies a condition of the loan.

- **Life insurance** that covers the outstanding balance. If something happens to you, the loan is cleared and your family keeps the home. The bank will offer its own policy. You can often use an external one if it meets their terms, which can be cheaper.
- **Property insurance** on the building structure. In many towers this is partly covered by the building's own policy through the service charge, but the bank still wants to see cover.

Budget a few thousand dirhams a year for both. It is not optional, so put it in your numbers from day one.

## When your fixed rate ends: switching banks

Here is where residents leave the most money on the table.

When your fixed period ends, your rate jumps to the bank's reversion rate, EIBOR plus their margin. Most people just let it happen. Do not. Three months before your fix ends, compare:

1. **Stay and renegotiate.** Ask your bank for a new fixed deal. They often have one, but only if you ask.
2. **Switch banks (a buyout).** Another bank pays off your loan and gives you a new rate. You will pay a new arrangement fee, valuation and mortgage registration, plus the early settlement fee if you leave mid-term.
3. **Release equity.** If your home has gone up in value, you may be able to borrow more for another purchase.

On a AED 1.4 million balance, the difference between a reversion rate of EIBOR plus 1.99% and a fresh deal at EIBOR plus 1% is about 1% a year. That is roughly AED 14,000 a year in your pocket for one phone call.

## Self-employed? Here is what changes
Business owners get mortgages here every day. The bank just needs more proof.
- **Trading history.** Most banks want at least two years of trading under your licence.
- **Accounts.** Two years of audited financial statements, plus six to twelve months of business and personal bank statements.
- **Income.** Banks usually average your income over two years and may count only part of it. Pay yourself a consistent salary in the run-up.
- **Terms.** Some banks offer slightly lower loan-to-value or slightly higher rates to self-employed borrowers. A broker earns their fee here by knowing which banks like your profile.

## First-time buyer checklist
1. Check your debt burden: list every loan and card limit.
2. Close the credit cards you do not use.
3. Get a pre-approval before you view properties.
4. Decide how long you will really stay.
5. Set a budget that includes 5% to 7% in fees.
6. Compare fixed and variable, including reversion margins.
7. Ask whether a salary transfer earns a better rate.
8. Check the service charge history of the building.
9. Get the property valued before you commit to the price.
10. Diary your fixed-rate end date the day you sign.

## Documents for residents

- Passport, visa and Emirates ID
- Salary certificate and three to six months of payslips
- Six months of bank statements
- For self-employed: trade licence, two years of audited accounts and company statements
- Property details: MOU or reservation form

## Mistakes I see residents make
- **Buying for the visa, not the numbers.** The Golden Visa is a bonus. The property still has to make sense on rent, costs and resale.
- **Forgetting the service charge.** It can add thousands a year. Check the building's history before you fall in love with the view.
- **Maxing out the loan.** Just because the bank will lend 80% does not mean you should. Leave room for a rate rise and a quiet month.
- **Ignoring the fix end date.** The reversion rate is where banks make their money. Diary it and shop around.
- **Choosing the bank before the broker.** One bank sees one profile. A broker sees 18 plus lenders and knows which one likes yours.

## My take

Owning in the UAE is no longer just for investors. With 80% finance, a fixed rate and a plan to stay four or five years, many residents are paying a landlord's mortgage for them.

But do the maths properly. Count every fee, count every debt, stress test the rate, and think about how long you will really stay.
