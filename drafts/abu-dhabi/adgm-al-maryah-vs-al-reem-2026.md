Most people looking at Abu Dhabi ask the wrong question.

They ask: *"Should I buy near ADGM?"*

That's like asking "should I buy in London?" It's not one market. It's two. Two islands. Two buyers. Two completely different games. And if you don't know which game you're playing, you'll either overpay for scarcity or underestimate volume.

So let me give you the framework I use with clients. Simple. Numbers first. No brochure language.

## Key takeaways
- Aldar and Mubadala announced an AED 60bn+ expansion of Al Maryah Island in December 2025, adding about 450,000 sqm of Grade A offices, which roughly doubles the island's commercial space.
- Three new bridges will link Al Maryah to Al Reem Island and the mainland. Enabling works were scheduled to start in 2026.
- ADGM's workforce hit 49,027 in H1 2026, up 34% year on year. Assets under management rose 54%.
- On March 2026 listing data, Al Maryah averaged about AED 2,043 per sq ft and Al Reem about AED 1,497, and both returned about 6.8% gross.
- Al Maryah is the scarce core: brand, trophy assets and a long hold. Al Reem is the workforce engine: lower entry, more supply and more liquidity.

## The thesis in one line

**The state is building the boardroom on one island and the bedrooms on the other, and connecting them with bridges.**

That's it. That's the whole play. Everything else in this article is detail.

[ILLUSTRATION: Cinematic black and white aerial of the two islands side by side, with coordinates. Al Maryah labelled "THE CORE: offices, brands, hotels". Al Reem labelled "THE ENGINE: where the workforce lives". Three brass lines marking the planned bridges between them and to the mainland.]

## Why this is happening now

Let's start with the demand, because demand is the only thing that pays you.

ADGM's first half of 2026, from its own results:

- **Workforce: 49,027**, up **34%** year on year
- **4,688** people added in six months alone
- **Assets under management up 54%**
- **13,974** active licences, with operational entities up 34% to 3,986
- **190** fund and asset managers, with a record number added in a single quarter

[CHART: Stat tiles in the site's cinematic style: 49,027 workforce (+34%) · +54% AUM · 13,974 licences · 190 asset managers. Source: ADGM H1 2026 results.]

Read that again. Almost **5,000 new professionals in six months.** Fund managers, lawyers, compliance teams, bankers, founders. Most of them were relocated. Most of them need somewhere to live. And most of them want to be within 15 minutes of the office.

This isn't hype money. It's payroll money. And payroll money pays rent.

## The supply response: AED 60 billion on one island

In December 2025, Mubadala and Aldar launched a joint venture to develop the north side of Al Maryah Island, the island's last major undeveloped landbank. Aldar holds 60% and Mubadala 40%.

The headline numbers:

| What | Size |
|---|---|
| Gross development value | More than AED 60 billion |
| Land | Nearly 500,000 sqm on the island's north side |
| Total floor space | About 1.5 million sqm |
| New Grade A offices | About 450,000 sqm, roughly doubling the island's commercial capacity |
| Homes | More than 3,000 luxury residences |
| Connectivity | Three new bridges to Al Reem and the mainland, 2.5 km of air-conditioned walkways, 12,000+ parking spaces |
| Open space | About 20% of the site |

[CHART: Before and after bar. Al Maryah Grade A office stock today vs after the expansion (about double). Label "450,000 sqm added". Source: Aldar and Mubadala announcement, December 2025.]

Now here is the number that matters for investors.

**More than 3,000 new homes on the core island. Against a workforce that grew by 4,688 in half a year.**

The core is not being built to house everyone. It's being built to house the people at the top, and to put the offices and hotels where the capital is. Everyone else lives across the water.

Which brings us to the second island.

## Al Reem: already inside ADGM

Here is the bit most people miss. Al Reem Island isn't "becoming" part of the financial district. It already is.

Cabinet Resolution No. 41 of 2023 extended ADGM's jurisdiction to Al Reem Island. That took ADGM's footprint to roughly **1,438 hectares, about ten times its previous size.** Overnight, ADGM went from one of the smallest international financial centres in the world to one of the biggest by area.

So when the state builds three bridges between Al Maryah and Al Reem, it isn't linking two separate places. It's knitting one financial district together.

## The twist: same yield, different game

You'd expect the cheaper island to win on yield. That's the textbook answer. It's also wrong, at least right now.

Bayut's listing data from March 2026:

| | Al Maryah Island | Al Reem Island |
|---|---|---|
| Average asking price | About AED 2,043 per sq ft | About AED 1,497 per sq ft |
| Average asking rent | About AED 138 per sq ft a year | About AED 102 per sq ft a year |
| Gross yield | **About 6.8%** | **About 6.8%** |

[CHART: Two bars for price per sq ft (2,043 vs 1,497) with a single shared line marked "6.8% gross yield on both". Source: Bayut listing data, March 2026. Note: asking prices and rents, not transactions.]

Same gross yield. Completely different investments.

On **Al Maryah**, the tenant pays a premium to live next to the office, and the scarcity is engineered. The building, the brand and the view do a lot of the work.

On **Al Reem**, you get in about 27% cheaper per square foot. There is far more stock, far more choice and far more turnover. The income is driven by volume.

So the question isn't "which yields more?" It's: **which kind of return do you want, and which kind of risk can you live with?**

One warning. Gross is not net. Service charges vary wildly from building to building, and in some Al Reem towers they can eat a big chunk of the rent. Always underwrite the building, not the island average.

## AED 2 million on each island: the maths

Let's make it real. Same budget, AED 2 million, on each island, using the March 2026 averages.

| | Al Maryah | Al Reem |
|---|---|---|
| Space you buy | About 979 sq ft | About 1,336 sq ft |
| Gross rent a year | About AED 135,100 | About AED 136,300 |
| Service charge at an illustrative AED 20 per sq ft | About AED 19,600 | About AED 26,700 |
| Rent after service charge | About AED 115,500 | About AED 109,600 |
| Yield after service charge | **About 5.8%** | **About 5.5%** |

[CHART: Grouped bars for each island: gross rent vs rent after service charge, with the yields labelled. Note "illustrative, AED 20 per sq ft service charge applied to both; real charges vary by building".]

See what happened? On Al Reem you get more space for your money. Great. But service charges are billed per square foot, so the extra space costs you more to run. The gross yields were identical. The net yields aren't.

That doesn't make Al Reem worse. A building with lower service charges, a better layout or stronger rents flips this table straight back. It just proves my rule: **underwrite the building, not the island.**

## The two playbooks

Here's how I break it down for clients.

| | Al Maryah: the core | Al Reem: the engine |
|---|---|---|
| What you're buying | Scarcity, brand, address | Volume, demand, access |
| Typical buyer | Family offices, senior executives, long-term investors | Buy-to-let investors, first-time Abu Dhabi buyers, end users |
| Entry price | Higher, about AED 2,000+ per sq ft | Lower, about AED 1,500 per sq ft |
| What drives the return | Limited supply, brand premium, corporate tenants | Workforce growth, steady demand, liquidity |
| Main risk | Paying too much for a logo, smaller resale pool | Supply competition, service charges, tenant churn |
| Typical hold | 7 to 10 years | 3 to 5 years, with a refinance or resale exit |

**Want scarce, long-term equity?** Al Maryah.
**Want cash flow, choice and an easier exit?** Al Reem.
**Want both?** That's what a lot of my clients end up doing. One of each.

## The branded residence trap on Al Maryah

Al Maryah is where Abu Dhabi's big branded towers are landing: W Residences, St. Regis, The Ritz-Carlton Residences and Jumeirah Residences, among others.

But remember the number from my [branded residences analysis](/insights/abu-dhabi-vs-miami-branded-residences-2026/): **92% of Abu Dhabi's branded pipeline is standalone, with no hotel attached.** Globally, it's 37%.

Savills puts the average branded premium at about **33%**, and that's mostly a launch premium. The brands that hold it will be the ones with real service and real distribution. Marriott Bonvoy, behind W, St. Regis and Ritz-Carlton, passed **295 million members** in 2026. That matters. But it doesn't replace due diligence.

On Al Maryah, the brand is part of what you're paying for. Make sure someone is still paying for it when you sell.

## The bridge play

Three new bridges. That's the detail I'd underline twice.

Bridges do two things for investors.

**One, they make it one labour market.** If you can work in an ADGM tower and be home on Al Reem in minutes, the whole district behaves like a single city centre. That supports rents on both sides.

**Two, they create a price gap you can use.** Buy where it's cheaper, close to the new bridge landings, and benefit from the core's growth without paying the core's price.

This isn't a new idea. It's how every mature financial centre grew:

- **Manhattan and Jersey City.** The core and the commuter towers across the water.
- **The City of London and Canary Wharf.** The legacy core and the newer expansion.
- **Hong Kong Island and Kowloon.** Premium offices, dense residential.

Abu Dhabi is running the same playbook. The difference is that it's doing it on purpose, in one decade, with no personal income tax and a currency pegged to the US dollar.

## What this means if you're buying off-plan

Most off-plan buyers look at one project in isolation. Don't.

- **Al Maryah sets the benchmark.** It's where price discovery happens for the whole district.
- **Al Reem absorbs the volume.** It's where the workforce that can't fit on Al Maryah ends up.

So if you're buying off-plan on Al Reem, watch how fast the Al Maryah offices fill. If you're buying on Al Maryah, watch how much new supply is landing on Al Reem, because that's your tenant's alternative.

And remember who you sell to. Before completion you're selling to other investors. At completion, mortgage buyers and end users arrive, and in a district like this, many of them work across the bridge. ## The risks, said plainly

I'd be doing you a disservice if I only sold the dream.

- **Delivery risk.** The expansion is a multi-year programme, and bridges and towers can slip. Price in delays.
- **Supply risk on Al Reem.** More stock means more competition for tenants. Choose buildings with a clear edge, such as a view, a layout or proximity to the bridges.
- **Brand risk on Al Maryah.** Not every branded building will hold its premium without a hotel behind it.
- **Data risk.** Asking prices are not sale prices. Check registered transactions with ADREC data before you commit.
- **Regional context.** Abu Dhabi is very stable day to day, but read the news on the wider region and make your own call.

## My take

ADGM isn't a 12-month flip. It's a ten-year capital cycle, backed by a sovereign fund, the emirate's biggest developer and a workforce growing by thousands every quarter.

The investors who win here won't be the ones who picked the "right island." They'll be the ones who understood the relationship between the two: the core that sets the price, the engine that houses the people, and the bridges that connect them.

So don't ask, "Should I buy near ADGM?"

Ask: **"Which side of the two-island play fits my money, and my timeline?"**
