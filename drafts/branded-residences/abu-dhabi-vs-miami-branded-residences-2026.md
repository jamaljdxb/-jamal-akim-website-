A brand on a building is a promise.

The only question that matters is this: **is anyone still keeping that promise the day you sell?**

Because that is what you are paying for. Not the logo. Not the lobby. The promise that someone will still want this apartment, at a premium, three years after the keys.

Abu Dhabi just became one of the biggest branded residence markets on earth. Miami has been one for years. So let me show you what is really going on, where the opportunity is, and where people are going to get burned.

## Key takeaways
- Abu Dhabi has 31 branded residence projects: 5 completed and 26 in the pipeline, sixth globally (Global Branded Residences, 2026).
- 92% of Abu Dhabi's pipeline is standalone with no hotel, against 37% globally.
- Savills puts the average branded premium at about 33%, mostly measured at launch.
- ADGM's workforce rose 34% and its assets under management 54% in H1 2026, which drives demand on Al Maryah Island.
- Compared with Miami, Abu Dhabi offers lower entry prices, no annual property tax and a dollar-linked currency.


## The headline: Abu Dhabi is now top six in the world

Global Branded Residences' 2026 report, *The Brand Standard*, puts Abu Dhabi at:

- **31** branded residence projects in total
- **5** completed
- **26** in the pipeline
- **#6** city market in the world by project count
- Pipeline **joint third** globally, alongside Cairo, behind only **Dubai** and **Miami**

[CHART: Horizontal bars, branded residence pipeline by city (projects): Dubai (largest, label "No. 1"), Miami ("No. 2"), Abu Dhabi 26 and Cairo 26 (joint 3rd). Source: Global Branded Residences, The Brand Standard 2026. Only label exact counts we have; show Dubai and Miami as rank labels.]

Knight Frank counts it differently, 24 schemes with 19 in the pipeline, so do not mix the two sets of numbers. But both say the same thing. Abu Dhabi has gone from a handful of branded buildings to a serious global market in about three years.

On the surface, that is a buy signal.

Now here is the part nobody puts in the brochure.

## The catch: 92% have no hotel

**92%** of Abu Dhabi's branded pipeline is **standalone**. That means a brand on the building, but no hotel in it.

Globally, that number is **37%**.

[CHART: Two donut charts side by side. Abu Dhabi pipeline: 92% standalone, 8% hotel-attached. Global projects: 37% standalone, 63% hotel-attached. Source: Global Branded Residences 2026.]

Why does that matter so much?

Because the whole reason branded residences earned a premium in the first place was the hotel. The concierge who already knows your name. Housekeeping on demand. The restaurant downstairs. The global standard you trust in Paris, Bali or Miami.

When there is no hotel, you are relying on the brand's residential operating team to deliver that standard on its own. Some do it brilliantly. Some are a name on the façade and a nicer font on the service charge invoice.

Same logo. Very different asset.

## The premium is real, but it is not a guarantee

Savills puts the average branded premium at around **33%** over comparable non-branded homes. Around **30%** in established cities. Around **39%** in resort locations.

[CHART: Bar chart, average branded premium: established cities ~30%, global average ~33%, resort locations ~39%. Source: Savills, Branded Residences 2025/26.]

Two things to understand about that number.

**One. It is mostly a launch premium.** It measures what developers achieve when they sell. Nobody publishes a clean, global number for what these units resell for years later. That is the number you actually live with.

**Two. It is an average.** Averages hide winners and losers. The brands with real service, real operators and real demand hold their premium. The ones without it slide back towards the price of the building next door.

> You don't make money on the premium you pay. You make money on the premium the next buyer pays.

But even here, I would ask the standalone questions before I paid anything:

- Is there a Ritz-Carlton hotel operating in the building or the development, or is this a residences-only operation?
- Who runs the residential services day to day, and for how long is that contract?
- What exactly does the service charge buy, and how does it compare with W, St. Regis and Jumeirah on the same island?
- What will the 60% at handover look like with a mortgage, at the rates we have today?

## Abu Dhabi vs Miami: the honest comparison

Miami is the city everyone compares Dubai and Abu Dhabi with. Branded towers, waterfront, sun, global money. Savills ranks Miami's branded pipeline second only to Dubai. So how does Abu Dhabi stack up?

| | Abu Dhabi | Miami |
|---|---|---|
| Currency | Dirham, pegged to USD at 3.6725 | US dollar |
| Branded pipeline | 26 projects, joint 3rd globally | 2nd globally (Savills) |
| Entry price, big-brand residence | Ritz-Carlton Al Maryah from about AED 4m (USD 1.1m) | St. Regis Brickell from about USD 5m |
| Annual property tax | None for owners | Non-homestead taxed on full value, often around 1.7% to 2% a year |
| Tax on rental income | No personal income tax | US federal tax, plus filings, for foreign owners |
| Selling as a foreigner | Standard transfer fees | 15% FIRPTA withholding on the sale price, reconciled later |
| Estate exposure | No inheritance tax; register a will | US estate tax can apply to non-residents' US property above USD 60,000 |
| Building costs to watch | Service charges | HOA fees, plus higher reserves and assessments under Florida's post-Surfside condo laws, and hurricane insurance |
| Residency | Golden Visa from AED 2m of property | Property alone does not give residency |

[CHART: Simple cost-of-holding illustration on a USD 2 million branded unit for 10 years. Miami: property tax at about 1.8% = USD 36,000 a year, USD 360,000 over 10 years (before HOA and insurance). Abu Dhabi: USD 0 property tax. Label "illustrative, excludes service charges, HOA and insurance".]

Here is the bit that changes the maths. On a USD 2 million Miami condo, annual property tax around 1.8% is roughly **USD 36,000 a year**. Over ten years, that is about **USD 360,000** before HOA, insurance or a single repair. In Abu Dhabi, that line is **zero**.

Miami has deeper history, a bigger resale track record and a huge domestic buyer pool. That matters. But for an international buyer comparing the two, Abu Dhabi gives you a dollar-linked asset, at a lower entry price, without the annual tax bill or the US estate exposure. That is why I keep pointing clients at Abu Dhabi.

## Own vs hotel: do the maths yourself

You will hear people say a branded apartment is "cheaper than a hotel after X nights." Sometimes true. Often oversold. Here is the honest way to work it out.

**Your real annual cost of owning** = what your money would earn elsewhere + service charge + other running costs, minus any rent you earn when you are not there.

**Break-even nights** = that annual cost ÷ the nightly rate you would otherwise pay.

An illustrative example:

- AED 4 million unit, bought cash
- Opportunity cost at 4%: AED 160,000 a year
- Service charge and running costs: say AED 45,000 a year
- Annual cost before rent: **AED 205,000**
- At AED 1,500 a night in a comparable hotel: about **137 nights** to break even
- Rent it out long term at 6% gross for most of the year, and the maths flips completely

[CHART: Simple bar or formula graphic showing the calculation above. Label "illustrative; plug in your own numbers".]

The point is not the exact number. The point is that branded residences work best as **investments you can also use**, not hotel replacements you hope will appreciate.

## My five-point screen before I pay any brand premium

1. **Hotel or no hotel?** If it is standalone, the brand's residential service has to stand on its own. Ask to see it working in a completed building.
2. **Who operates, and for how long?** A brand-managed building with a long operating agreement is a different asset from a name licensed for marketing.
3. **What does the service charge buy?** Get the line items. Compare with the branded tower next door and a top non-branded tower.
4. **What is the resale evidence?** Look at the brand's completed UAE buildings two to three years after handover. Premium held, or premium gone?
5. **Who buys it next?** On Al Maryah, that is ADGM executives and their landlords. Make sure the layout, size and price suit them, not just the brochure.

If a project passes all five, the premium can be worth every dirham. If it fails two, you are paying for a logo.

Abu Dhabi's branded residence boom is real, and Al Maryah Island has the demand to back it. But 92% of the pipeline has no hotel, and the average premium is a launch number, not a promise.

The winners will be the brands that actually deliver service, in the buildings ADGM's workforce actually wants to live in. Compared with Miami, Abu Dhabi gives you a lower entry price, no annual property tax and a dollar-linked currency. That is a strong hand. Just play it with data, not with a logo.
